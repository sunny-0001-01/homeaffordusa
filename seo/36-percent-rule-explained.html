36% Rule Explained for Total Debt

Short answer: Your ability to buy a home in the United States depends on income, debt, savings, credit score, and location. This guide explains everything step by step.

Why This Matters

Most buyers underestimate how much their financial profile affects affordability. Mortgage approval is not the same as financial comfort.

Income vs Real Affordability

Lenders typically approve loans between 3–4× annual income, but this ignores taxes, insurance, maintenance, and lifestyle costs.

Down Payment Impact

Putting 20% down avoids PMI and lowers monthly payments. A 10% down payment allows faster buying but increases risk and cost.

Debt Changes Everything

Student loans, car payments, and credit cards reduce buying power through the 36% total debt rule.

Property Taxes and Location

High-tax states dramatically increase monthly ownership cost. Texas, Illinois, and New Jersey are common examples.

Renting vs Buying

Renting may feel cheaper short-term, but buying builds equity and protects against rising rents. However, buying too early can hurt finances.

Credit Score Requirements

Most conventional loans require 620+. Better credit unlocks lower interest rates and higher affordability.

How to Improve Your Buying Power

  • Increase income
  • Reduce consumer debt
  • Save aggressively
  • Improve credit score
  • Choose affordable markets

Realistic Buyer Timeline

Most households need several years to save for a down payment unless income is very high.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I wait to buy? Depends on job stability and interest rates.

Is PMI bad? It increases cost but enables earlier ownership.

How much house can I afford? Usually 3–4× income.
