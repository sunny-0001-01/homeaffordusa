Can I Afford a $300,000 House on $60,000 Salary?

Short answer: It depends on your savings rate, debt, and monthly expenses. Below is a complete breakdown using lender rules and realistic assumptions.

Income Breakdown

If you earn $60,000 per year, your gross monthly income is approximately $5,000. Most lenders recommend that your housing costs stay under 28% of this amount, which equals about $1,400 per month.

Down Payment Requirement

A traditional 20% down payment on a $300,000 home equals $60,000. If you are saving aggressively, this may take several years unless your income increases significantly.

Mortgage & Monthly Ownership Cost

Beyond the mortgage principal and interest, you must account for property taxes, homeowners insurance, maintenance, and potential HOA fees. Many buyers underestimate these costs, which can add hundreds of dollars per month.

Timeline To Buy

If you save 20% of your income annually, reaching a 20% down payment could take 5–10 years depending on lifestyle and debt obligations. Increasing income or lowering your target price dramatically shortens this timeline.

Lender Rules Explained

The 28% rule limits housing costs to 28% of gross income. The 36% rule includes total debt payments. If you have car loans or student loans, your maximum affordable home price decreases.

How To Improve Affordability

  • Increase household income
  • Reduce high-interest debt
  • Move to a lower property-tax state
  • Lower your target purchase price
  • Increase down payment beyond 20%

Is $60,000 Enough For a $300,000 House?

For many buyers, lenders typically approve loans between 3x–4x annual income. That means a $60,000 income often supports a home between $180,000 and $240,000. Compare this to your $300,000 target carefully.

State Taxes & Location Impact

Property taxes vary widely by state. High-tax states significantly increase monthly ownership cost, reducing how much home you can comfortably afford.

Realistic Recommendation

If your expenses are high, this price may stretch your budget. A slightly lower home price may create better long-term financial stability.
